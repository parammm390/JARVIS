# JARVIS Marketing — Brand Voice & Copy Standard

This is the written reference every phase of `JARVIS-MARKETING-MAESTRO-PLAN.md` writes copy against. If a headline, subhead, card, or CTA doesn't survive this document, it gets rewritten — not shipped with a caveat.

## The one-sentence brief

JARVIS turns an instruction into an approved, executed, evidenced business action for a water-treatment dealer. Every sentence on the site should make that more real to the reader than the last one did — not describe a feature, prove the mechanic.

## Voice pillars

**Exact.** Say the real mechanism, not a category. Not "AI-powered automation" — "the invoice is drafted, then it waits for you to approve it." Vague power-words (*seamless, revolutionary, cutting-edge, next-generation, game-changing, effortless*) are banned outright — they're what a company says when it doesn't want to say what the thing actually does.

**Accountable.** JARVIS's whole design is an evidence trail — a receipt for every decision, a policy version behind every price, a human gate before every side effect. The copy should read like it was written by something that has nothing to hide, because the product doesn't. When in doubt, cite the mechanism instead of the outcome ("it quotes from your price book" beats "smarter quotes").

**Unshowy but alive.** The visual system (Orb, living water, receipts) is already striking — the copy doesn't need adjectives doing the same job. Confidence reads as short sentences and a straight face, not exclamation points.

**Precise about limits.** JARVIS is not a chatbot, not a CRM, not an unattended autonomous agent. Say what it isn't as plainly as what it is — that's a credibility move here, not a hedge, because the product's own design (approval gates, honest labels, disqualification language already in the FAQ) makes the boundary a real, demonstrable thing, not marketing caution.

## Hard rules

1. **Never "AI receptionist."** Not as a synonym anywhere, not in alt text, not in a testimonial voice. If a sentence needs that phrase to make sense, the sentence is wrong, not the rule.
2. **No invented numbers.** Every stat must trace to something real — a computed value (the missed-call calculator), a documented guarantee (response/launch/route guarantees), or a real capability count. A "reduces no-shows by 40%"-style placeholder never ships, even as an illustrative example.
3. **Ask, don't decide, when honesty requires a hedge.** If a claim needs a footnote to stay true, either make the claim plain enough that it doesn't need one, or cut the claim. Never bury a hedge in fine print under an overclaiming headline. This mirrors the product's own rule: JARVIS asks before it acts; the copy about JARVIS should have the same discipline about what it claims.
4. **Keep the disqualification beat.** Somewhere real (today: FAQ's "who this is NOT for," the boundaries/guardrails section) the site says plainly who this isn't for. Never remove it to make the pitch sound bigger than the product.
5. **Technically accurate is the floor, not the bar.** A sentence that's true but forgettable gets redrafted. Read every headline out loud before it ships — if it sounds like every other B2B SaaS site, it's not done.
6. **Voice-agent demotion, exactly.** The after-hours phone-answering capability is real, stays in the product, and is never the headline, a tier name, or the first thing a section leads with. Its total surface area across the whole rebuilt site: one line in "the loop" section (equal visual/copy weight to text and typed-command as input channels), one FAQ question, one Pricing line-item. If a draft gives it more room than that anywhere, cut it back.

## Worked examples — the actual bar

**Hero, before (current site):**
> "The job you lose tonight is booked with your competitor by morning."

Correctly urgent, but it's a call-answering company's headline, not JARVIS's. Reframe:

> **"Give it an instruction. Watch it ask before it acts."**
> Every quote, every invoice, every reschedule — planned in seconds, drafted against your real price book, and held for your yes before anything happens. Approve it and there's a receipt. Reject it and nothing ran.

**A capability card, weak draft:**
> "AI-Powered Smart Scheduling — Our advanced AI optimizes your technician routes for maximum efficiency."

Category words doing no work. Reframe:

> "It doesn't guess a route. It compares every real option against drive time and load, and tells you exactly how many kilometers the naive route would have cost you."

**The memory beat, weak draft:**
> "Never lose a customer's history again with our powerful CRM integration."

Reframe (pulling the Lifecycle demo's own line, which already clears this bar):

> **"The call was minute one. This is the next two years."**
> One record for the household — the water test, the quote, the job, the reminder, the referral — kept the same way whether you look at it in a week or in three years.

**FAQ voice question — the one place voice-calling appears with any explanation:**
> "Does it work over the phone? — Voice is one of the ways an instruction reaches JARVIS. A call, a text, or someone typing it in — the plan it drafts and the approval it waits for are exactly the same either way."

Note the shape: voice gets a real, respectful, one-paragraph answer — and zero extra emphasis, zero separate section, zero pricing-tier name.

## Self-audit checklist (run before marking any copy task done)

- [ ] Would this sentence be true of literally any AI SaaS product? If yes, it's not specific enough — name the actual mechanism.
- [ ] Does any sentence say or imply "AI receptionist," "virtual receptionist," or "answering service" as the core identity?
- [ ] Is every number on this page traceable to something real?
- [ ] If voice/phone answering appears, is it exactly one line/mention, same weight as other channels — not a headline, not a tier name?
- [ ] Read the headline out loud. Does it sound like this company, or like every other one?
- [ ] Does the section make one thing more real to the reader than it was before they read it — not just describe a feature?
